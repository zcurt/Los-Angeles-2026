The Route

Wednesday, August 19

Hollywood and the World Science Fiction Convention

10:00 PM

Susan and I have never traveled to Los Angeles. Well, already that's only a half-truth. I have been but "Susan and I" have not. Then again, I was 5 at the time — does that count? Our family drove out from Galesburg, Illinois in a late 1960s station wagon. Dad always owned a station and a sedan. I've carried along that tradition.

We stopped at the Grand Canyon and Las Vegas. From my vantage point walking between the one level hotel to the outside pool and back again, Las Vegas seemed like a small, hot, dusty stopover. Except for the spinning lady.

We went to Circus, Circus for dinner. A woman held onto a device attached to the ceiling by biting it. She set herself to spinning and was raised up to the ceiling where she proceeded around the room — all the while spinning and attached only be her teeth.

But I digress. My purpose for going to Los Angelos is to attend the World Science Fiction Convention. I've always wanted to go to one and retirement provided the opportunity. It was clear that since one of us was on the west coast, two would be even better. So Susan and I decided to check out LA as tourists — prepending the convention with Hollywood, Museums, kayaking in the Pacific, and, of course, a jazz club or two.

Art of the Manuscript

1:06 PM

Our first stop in the museum was an exhibition on early manuscripts. They described the process from scraping skins to applying gold leaf. Shown is a page from the French Book of Chess Problems, made in the late 1300s—roughly 1375–1400. It was painted and written in tempera colors and gold leaf on calfskin parchment.

I'm just amazed that in the 1300s, given the difficulty of producing books one at time, that someone decided it was important to produce a book on chess problems.

As many of the photos included in this travel journal, a photo of this painting doesn't reveal it's impact nor contrast within the room

Art with simple media

2:16 PM

Another memorable painting was about men turning to stone. I enjoy how just a few elements can be put on paper to render a complex image. Scenes that were never real but imagined by their artist also seem to generate an emotional reaction in me. Often in museums they are scenes from hell but here it is Greek Mythology.

I find art with an undertone of the macabre strikes an emotion of wonder and respect for the imagination that developed it. I had a similar reaction to Redon's art shown later.

The Art of Redon

2:07 PM

There was a whole section devoted to the art of Odilon Redon. Much of his work is in pencil or charcoal on colored paper. That's already a medium I enjoy. Add to that his interest in drawing fantastical creatures or scenes hits me where I like it. Here is an interpretation of something from Edgar Alan Poe's Telltale Heart. It isn't the heart but the eye that was bothering the antagonist in this story and that's what Redon decided to draw. I find this irresistible.

Warner Bros

12:59 PM

We toured the Warner Bros studio. We spent most of the time on the streets of the studio reviewing old film locations. Although the history was interesting, most of the buildings were only facades with their interesting bits removed. It makes sense, of course that signs, street numbers, front lights, and other ephemera would be removed — to be used elsewhere and to force new scenes to update their image. But, it makes for less interesting visuals since nothing matches your memories.

Even the famous Warner Bros water tower was wrapped to promote their new streaming series. We did get to stop in to the props building. There were a lot of props. So many variations of the same thing (like chandeliers).

I was hoping to see more props that I would recognize but as this was a working building, there were just a few notable props sitting out and displayed for tourists.

The Pitt was in production, filming in one of the studio buildings. We saw some "patients" and I may have seen the back of Taylor Dearden Cranston. We couldn't take any pictures in that are but later I got one of the Pittsburgh ambulances. We did get to enjoy reproductions of Friends, Big Bang Theory, and Harry Potter sets.
